What could it be, and what is it confused with?

Upload a photo of a mushroom. The lens suggests species it might be, and the poisonous species those are often mistaken for.

The lens never tells you a mushroom is safe to eat. A photo cannot show spore colour, smell, the base of the stem or the other features experts check, and the AI makes mistakes. Deadly mushrooms look like edible ones. Never eat a wild mushroom unless an expert has identified it in person.

Drop a photo here
A clear photo of the cap, the gills and the stem works best.

The photo is shrunk in your browser and sent to Google Gemini. This site does not keep it, but Google may use it to improve its products, so do not upload anything private. The camera turns off as soon as you take the photo.

Possible matches

Suggested by AI from the photo, then checked against iNaturalist. The lens does not show whether any of them is edible.

Dangerous lookalikes

Poisonous species that iNaturalist observers often confuse with the possible matches. If any of these could be what you found, treat your mushroom as poisonous.

Or paste a DNA sequence

Its dangerous lookalikes

How it works: your sequence is cut into every 8-letter piece (a k-mer). The barcodes this site has downloaded from GenBank are ranked by how many pieces they share, on both DNA strands, and the closest few are aligned letter by letter. Identity is the share of aligned positions that match; species usually differ by more than 3%. Only barcodes this site already holds can be found, so the right species may be missing. Like the lens, it never says a mushroom is safe to eat.
