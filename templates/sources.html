Where everything comes from

Species data comes from public databases. Each record used is linked below.

Databases

NCBI GenBank

DNA barcode sequences. Each accession number in the table links to the record used.

NCBI Taxonomy

Family, order and the other classification ranks.

iNaturalist

Common names and photos. Photos are used under the Creative Commons licence shown for each.

GBIF

Where each species has been recorded. The country counts are a snapshot. The maps load live from GBIF.

Google Gemini

An AI that turns descriptions and photos into species names, which are then checked against iNaturalist. It also writes the quick facts and preparation steps on species pages, marked as AI-written. It never sets an edibility label.

Search pages

Species outside the five pairs are looked up live: names, photos and lookalikes from iNaturalist, summaries and edibility labels from Wikipedia, barcodes from GenBank. Lookalikes are the species observers most often confuse, counted from corrected identifications.

Licences

Photos

From iNaturalist observers under Creative Commons licences. Each photo names its author and licence: under the species photo, and when you hover over smaller photos.

Text

Species summaries are from Wikipedia under CC BY-SA 4.0, linked to each article.

Maps

Base maps © OpenStreetMap contributors and OpenMapTiles, served by GBIF. Records from GBIF under CC0 and CC BY.

DNA and classification

NCBI GenBank and Taxonomy data are public and free to use.

Paintings

Shown faintly at the top of each page, all public domain via Wikimedia Commons: Van Gogh, Undergrowth with Two Figures (1890) and Trees and Undergrowth (1887); Monet, The Bodmer Oak (1865); Courbet, Stream in the Forest (1862).

Plates

The hand-coloured mushrooms beside some sections are from James Sowerby, Coloured Figures of English Fungi or Mushrooms (1797–1815), public domain via Wikimedia Commons. They decorate the page and are not identification guides for the species beside them.
